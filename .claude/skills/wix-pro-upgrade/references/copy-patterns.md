# Copy patterns that read professional

## Homepage hero
```
H1:   <Outcome> for <audience> — <how, in ≤ 6 words>
Sub:  One sentence: the specific benefit and the proof (years, clients, certification, guarantee — only if true).
CTA:  <Verb> <object>   e.g. "Book a free assessment" · "See the collection" · "Get a quote in 24h"
```
Bad → good:
- "Welcome to our website" → "Precision aluminium fabrication for Riyadh contractors — delivered on schedule"
- "We offer many services" → "Three services. One point of contact. Fixed prices."
- "Contact us" → "Talk to an engineer today"

## Section headlines
Lead with the visitor's question, answer it in the body.
- "How it works" → three numbered steps, ≤ 12 words each.
- "Why us" → three proof points with a concrete detail each; no adjectives without evidence.
- "Services" → name + one-line outcome + starting price or "from" if pricing is public.

## Micro-copy
- Buttons: verb + object. Never "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" alone.
- Form success: say what happens next and when ("We reply within one business day").
- 404: apologise once, offer the three most useful links, keep the brand voice.
- Footer tagline: the H1 idea in ≤ 8 words.

## Tone controls
Pick one register and hold it site-wide: **Expert-calm** (clinics, law, engineering) · **Warm-direct** (hospitality, retail, wellness) · **Bold-minimal** (design, tech, fashion). Mixing registers reads amateur faster than any typo.

## Arabic / English bilingual
- Do not machine-translate headlines; brief each language separately with the same intent.
- Check that RTL layout mirrors icons/arrows and that numerals follow the site's convention consistently.
- Keep CTAs the same length in both languages so buttons match.

## Never
- Fabricate numbers, awards, testimonials, or client names.
- Use "world-class", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "solutions" without a specific noun.
- Leave placeholder or template text anywhere, including hidden pages and the 404.
